Plants for the Shade Garden

Six perennials and two ground covers that thrive in full shade. All are hardy in UK conditions, low-maintenance once established, and return reliably year after year.

Hostas

Hosta spp.

The structural centrepiece of any shade planting. Leaves range from the size of a playing card to a dinner plate, in greens, blues, golds, and variegated patterns. Once established, they are nearly indestructible and grow larger each year. Plant in spring or early autumn into rich, moisture-retentive soil. Watch for slugs in spring as new leaves emerge.

Conditions
Full shade to part shade, moist soil
Flowers
Tall spikes of white or lavender bells in summer
Height
15cm to 90cm depending on variety
Hardiness
Fully hardy, dies back each winter

Recommended varieties

  • 'Sum and Substance' giant gold-green
  • 'Halcyon' blue-grey, slug resistant
  • 'June' gold centre, blue margin
  • 'Frances Williams' bold ribbed, two-tone

Ferns

Athyrium & Dryopteris spp.

The structural backbone of any shade planting. Ferns evolved beneath forest canopies and are extremely good at thriving in low light. They give texture and movement no other plant family offers. Athyrium (lady fern) and Dryopteris (male fern) are the most reliable hardy choices for UK gardens.

Conditions
Full shade, moist soil with good leaf mould
Flowers
None, valued for foliage
Height
30cm to 120cm
Hardiness
Fully hardy, most are deciduous

Hellebores

Helleborus orientalis

One of very few plants that flower in deep winter under a bare canopy. Nodding waxy blooms appear in burgundy, cream, pink, slate, and near-black from January through April, when nothing else dares. Long-lived, drought-tolerant once established, and remarkably unfussy. Cut back old leaves in late winter so flowers show clearly.

Conditions
Full to part shade, any decent soil
Flowers
January to April
Height
40cm to 60cm
Hardiness
Fully hardy and evergreen

Lily of the Valley

Convallaria majalis

A British shade-border classic. Spreads quietly via underground rhizomes to form a dense, weed-suppressing mat. Small white bell flowers in May are powerfully scented. Plant once and it will take care of itself indefinitely. Note: all parts are toxic if eaten, so keep away from pets and small children.

Conditions
Full to part shade, prefers moist but tolerant of dry
Flowers
White, fragrant, May
Height
15cm to 25cm
Hardiness
Fully hardy, dies back over winter

Astilbe

Astilbe spp.

Astilbe brings the only real vertical drama to the shade garden. Feathery plumes in white, pink, red, and magenta rise above fern-like foliage in midsummer. Needs reliable moisture to perform well: plant near a damp patch or water regularly in dry spells. Flowers can be left standing into autumn for a rusted, sculptural look.

Conditions
Part to full shade, consistently moist soil
Flowers
June to August, 6 to 8 weeks of bloom
Height
40cm to 120cm
Hardiness
Fully hardy, dies back over winter

Sweet Woodruff

Galium odoratum

A low, spreading ground cover that forms a dense green carpet beneath trees where grass refuses to grow. Tiny white flowers appear in late spring, and dried leaves smell of fresh hay. Excellent under shrubs and in dry shade, though it will spread enthusiastically where happy.

Conditions
Full to part shade, tolerates dry shade
Flowers
White, tiny, May to June
Height
15cm to 20cm
Spread
Vigorous, plant where you want coverage

Ajuga (Bugle)

Ajuga reptans

One of the most versatile ground covers for shade. Dark, sometimes bronze foliage looks attractive year-round, and short spikes of blue-purple flowers appear in spring. Spreads by runners, roots where it touches soil, and is virtually indestructible once it gets going. Excellent for suppressing weeds.

Conditions
Full to part shade, any soil
Flowers
Blue-purple spikes, April to June
Height
15cm in flower, 10cm foliage
Hardiness
Fully hardy and evergreen
